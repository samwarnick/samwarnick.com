For the last couple of years, I’ve withdrawn a bit and have been working mostly in isolation. I’ve been building things for just me and my wife, with code living in private repos. It’s been fine, but I feel like I could, and maybe should, be doing more. So, I’m trying something new. You’ve heard this before, but I want to take app making _slightly_ more seriously. I’m dusting off an old brand of mine—Lemon Point.

Lemon Point is my LLC that I’ve done some freelancing and other stuff with in the past. Now, it’s going to be where my “serious” projects live. I’ve got ideas. A mix of web apps and iOS apps and macOS apps.

I’ve update the Lemon Point logo and made a new home for it at [lemonpoint.net](https://lemonpoint.net). I like it and so does my wife.

![Screenshot of website with yellow background and dark gray lemon shape and text that says “Lemon Point: Artisinally crafted yak shaved apps & stuff.”](/media/media-2.png)

My goals for Lemon Point:

1. Build stuff carefully, thoughtfully, and deliberately.
2. Share stuff.

I’m hoping that by separating projects between me and Lemon Point, I will be able to have cleaner boundaries in my brain. I’ve blogged (too much) about my stuggles with side projects and maintaining my sanity. A primary goal for me is always to figure out how to build things substainably. I tried to [take a break from programming for 30 days](https://samwarnick.com/blog/taking-a-break-from-programming-for-fun/), but I lastest barely three weeks. I just started feeling the itch. But forcing myself to take a break was a good idea. I’m going to build that into my routine. I’m going to have two weeks on and one week off. Maybe longer breaks every quarter or something. I might also try and limit myself to how many hours I can put in each week. I don’t want to feel like I’m rushing. In an age of vibe coding, I think we need more people building slowly. I’m pretty darn slow.

I’m still figuring out how I will use AI at Lemon Point, but the intent is for everything to be “human built”. I’m not totally sure what that means anymore, but that’s the goal. I cannot say I will never use AI—I enjoy exploring and trying new workflows. My current thoughts are that I am not going to use AIs to write code for me on these projects. I find using agentic AI to write code unsatisfying. I currently use AI to research and debug, the same way I have used Google or StackOverflow. But I’m still thinking this over and I might take a more hardline stance in the future.

To share, I mainly want to help others learn. I have learned more from looking at other people’s projects on GitHub than just about any other source—including my computer science degeree. The plan right now is for everything under Lemon Point to be open source with the MIT license. I thought about other licenses that prevent people from competing with you or allow just self-hosting, but things just get complicated. MIT is permissive and simple. I don’t think code is the valuable part of a product anyway. If you think you can run things better than me, you’re probably right. Go ahead and take what I’ve done and do better. It’s a risk I’m willing to take. I want people to use and learn from what I make without worrying.

Will this be a huge mistake? We’ll see! I’ve started by open-sourcing ScreenCred and moved it over to my new GitHub organization.

I’m still figuring out other ways I can share. I’m not great at social media, but I do blog occasionally. Let me know if you have ideas.
